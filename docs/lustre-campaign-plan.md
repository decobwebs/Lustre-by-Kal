## THE PLAN IN SIMPLE WORDS (read this first)

**1. Who we are talking to.**
The person who buys the gifts at a company. Most of the time this is the **HR manager**. Her boss says "buy the end-of-year gifts, and don't make us look cheap." She is afraid of three things:
- the gift looking cheap,
- the gift arriving late,
- her staff laughing at it in their WhatsApp group.

**2. The big idea: "The Group Chat."**
The whole video happens inside an office WhatsApp group, on the day the gifts arrive. All the messages are in plain English.
- Staff joke: *"Please let it not be the usual 🙏"*
- Then real photos of Lustre gifts appear in the chat: the cup, the towel, the cap, the notebook.
- Someone sees their own name written on the gift card: *"They wrote my NAME 😭"*
- Everyone praises HR: *"HR, well done this year 👏🏾"*
- Someone from another company asks: *"Who is the vendor?"* HR shares Lustre's contact.

**3. The main line:** *"They'll talk about it. Make it good."*
It means: your staff will talk about the gift anyway, so give them something good to talk about.

**4. Why this idea suits what we have.**
We have no new videos, and our photos are phone photos. Inside a WhatsApp chat, phone photos look normal and real. So our weakness becomes our style.

**5. What we will make.**
- **One 30-second main video** (the group chat story).
- **Three 15-second videos**, one for each hamper:
  - **Radiant:** for gifts to important clients.
  - **Grace:** for staff, and it has no alcohol, so everyone can enjoy it.
  - **Bloom:** for suppliers and partners.
- **One short video recorded from the website**, showing how easy ordering is: choose hampers, send on WhatsApp, get an invoice, pay after we confirm.

- **Square and wide versions** for Facebook and LinkedIn.

**7. How people will trust us.**

- **Your company's logo can go on the items**, and **every card can carry the person's name**. The Lustre logo on our own cup, towel and cap shows buyers what their logo would look like.

**8. When.** Start now (late October), when companies decide their December gifts. We post something new each week until Christmas, with friendly "order early" reminders.

## Stage 3 — Insights

**1. The group-chat verdict**
- *Observation:* Staff photograph year-end gifts and discuss them in office WhatsApp groups.
- *Tension:* HR controls the budget, but staff decide whether it was a good gift.
- *Insight:* **A staff gift is judged in public, within hours, by the very people it was meant to please.**
- *For our content:* The payoff is the review in the chat.

**2. The forgettable hamper**
- *Observation:* The classic hamper is drinks and groceries in cellophane.
- *Tension:* People are grateful, but it's forgotten and easily given away.
- *Insight:* **People remember the gift they'd never buy for themselves, and still use months later.**
- *For our content:* Lead with what gets kept. The joke is about predictable gifts, **never** about groceries, which matter in a hard year and are in our hampers too.

**3. Trust is the real luxury**
- *Observation:* "Is this vendor real?"
- *Tension:* They want the beautiful gift but won't risk the company's money.
- *Insight:* **For a corporate buyer, the most valuable thing a vendor can show is that nothing will go wrong.**
- *For our content:* "Pay after we confirm, by invoice", the reference number, and the real ordering flow on the website.

**4. HR is the one at risk**
- *Observation:* If gifts arrive late, short or cheap, HR takes the blame.
- *Tension:* Giving gifts is meant to feel generous, but for the organiser it's a risk.
- *Insight:* **She isn't buying a hamper. She's buying a peaceful December and her good name.**
- *For our content:* HR is the hero, and the chat is her verdict.

**5. Your name on it**
- *Observation:* Company gifts at scale are anonymous.
- *Tension:* The company wants to say "we see you", but volume makes gifts generic.
- *Insight:* **A gift becomes personal the moment someone sees their own name on it.**
- *For our content:* The real Lustre tag with a name written on it is the emotional peak. Confirmed true for all three hampers.

**6. Thanks in a hard year**
- *Observation:* Practical items carry weight right now.
- *Tension:* Lavish feels out of touch; cheap feels stingy.
- *Insight:* **The most respectful gift is useful *and* a little indulgent.**
- *For our content:* Staples and treats together. Warm, not flashy.

**7. The pile in the client's office**
- *Observation:* Senior clients receive many hampers.
- *Tension:* A gift meant to stand out becomes one box among many.
- *Insight:* **Clients remember the gift they opened first, and who sent it.**
- *For our content:* The Radiant spot.

**8. The forward is the real sale**
- *Observation:* Company purchases start as forwards: "Can we do this for staff?"
- *Tension:* The forwarder needs price, contents and process at a glance.
- *Insight:* **The ad must work as a forward, not just a view.**
- *For our content:* The story ends with a forward ("Who is the vendor? Send me their number"). Every film ends on an end card that works as a forward, plus a still version.

## Stage 4 — Creative concepts (re-scored for the no-shoot constraint)

### A. "The Group Chat" — story + humour (Insights 1, 4, 5, 8)

**The idea:** The whole story plays out inside an office WhatsApp group on the morning the year-end gifts land. Real Lustre photos and clips arrive as messages, the group goes from "please let it not be the usual" to "they wrote my NAME 😭", and it ends with someone from another company asking for the vendor's number.

**Hero line:** **"They'll talk about it. Make it good."**

**Beats:**
1. Ping: "Hampers have arrived at reception 👀". A photo is still loading, blurred.
2. Banter: "Please let it not be the usual 🙏" / "Remember last year's? 😂"
3. A video message: the real courier bags.
4. A photo album: the real Lustre Stanley cup, towel, cap, notebook. "Even the towel has a logo 😂🙌🏾"
5. The real gift tag, with a name written on it. "They wrote my NAME 😭"
6. "HR, well done this year 👏🏾". HR replies "😌". Then "My sister's company wants this. Who is the vendor?" HR shares a contact: Lustre by Kal.
7. The tier cards, "Pay by invoice", WhatsApp.

**Emotional arc:** curiosity → recognition and a laugh → anticipation → delight → feeling seen → pride → "let me ask them".

**Why it works:**
- HR is the hero, and her fear (public judgment) becomes her reward.
- The format is native to Nigerian offices.
- The forward is built into the story.

**Objections removed:** "Will it look ordinary?" and "Is this vendor real?" (real items, then the invoice).

**Assets:** everything already exists. The low-res photos and clips are **at home** in a chat.

**Risks:**
- It's a dramatisation and must never look like real testimony. Fictional group, no real names, a "Dramatisation" note, and only items that really exist.
- Don't copy the WhatsApp logo or its exact UI.
- The humour targets predictability, never staff or groceries.

### 5.2 Five alternative hooks (first 2 seconds)

## Stage 7 — Measurement

### Test first: the 5 hooks
- Same body and end card, Click-to-WhatsApp.
- Small equal budgets for 3–4 days.
- Then the 3 tier spots, then the how-to-order film as a retargeting piece.

### Numbers to watch
- **Hook rate:** 3-second views ÷ impressions
- **Hold rate:** 15-second views ÷ 3-second views
- Average watch time
- **WhatsApp conversations started**
- **Cost per conversation**
- Enquiry → quote → paid invoice, tracked by the ad code and the LBK reference

Compare variants **against each other**. Don't chase benchmarks we can't verify.

### What to change based on results
- **Low hook rate:** change only the opening.
- **Good hook, poor hold:** trim 8–12s, the album.
- **Views but no chats:** a trust gap. Make "Pay by invoice" bigger and push the how-to-order film.
- **Chats but no sales:** a price or process issue. Push tier-mixing and give faster quotes.
- **One tier spot wins:** move budget to it, and reuse its angle on LinkedIn.
